# Changelog

All notable changes to VICTUS are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog 1.1.0](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and versions follow [Semantic Versioning 2.0.0](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html); before 1.0.0 a minor version may change public C++ APIs (see "Compatibility" in [docs/RELEASE_PROCESS.md](docs/RELEASE_PROCESS.md)). Project, scene and asset file formats carry their own version numbers and are migrated on load; any format change is listed under "Changed".

Every entry links to the evidence in [docs/STATUS.md](docs/STATUS.md); nothing here claims more than STATUS verifies. Platform and hardware limits are in [docs/KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md](docs/KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md).

## [Unreleased]

No VICTUS version has been released yet. The first tagged release will be 0.2.0 (the version in CMakeLists.txt) and will move this section under `## [0.2.0] - YYYY-MM-DD`.

### Added

- A Max Vertex Offset setting on materials (`max_vertex_offset` in `.vmat`, ADR-0245) grows culling bounds by the distance a shader's `vertex()` function moves geometry. Swaying trees, waves and other displaced meshes no longer pop out at the screen edges or lose their shadows. The starter foliage materials declare 0.5 m. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-24--culling-bounds-growth-for-vertex-functions-adr-0245))
- Animated vertex-function materials, such as the starter foliage wind, now get motion vectors and join the depth prepass (ADR-0244), so swaying leaves stay sharp under TAA instead of smearing and get SSAO. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--vertex-functions-in-the-depth-prepass-and-motion-vectors-adr-0244))
- Procedural trees that sway in the wind (ADR-0243): a `Tree` component builds a seeded broadleaf or conifer (trunk, branches and a soft-shaded crown, no textures) in Studio and in play, and the starter leaves and bark materials carry a foliage wind vertex shader (`assets/shaders/starter/foliage_wind.vshader`) whose sway reaches the shadows too. The 3D and multiplayer templates now have a ring of 22 trees on the meadow around the play area. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--procedural-trees-and-foliage-wind-adr-0243))
- A better-looking procedural city (ADR-0241): windows divided by piers and mullions, glossy glass that reflects the sky and the street, shopfronts with awnings, cornices, rooftop units, per-building colour variation and soft rounded trees. Glossy surfaces seen at a grazing angle no longer show a dither in their screen-space reflections. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--procedural-city-facades-glass-and-trees-ssr-fallback-adr-0241))
- Anti-tiling for repeating materials (ADR-0240): `.vmat` `anti_tiling` adds large-scale brightness variation and, for organic surfaces, a distant larger-scale blend, so grass, rock and paving no longer show a grid of repeats. The starter grass, rock, paving and planks use it. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--anti-tiling-for-repeating-materials-adr-0240))
- Sky-coloured fog (ADR-0239): Environment `fog_from_sky` makes distance fog take the HDR sky's colour behind each pixel, so distant ground fades into the horizon. The 3D, no-code 3D and multiplayer templates use it, with a wide grass backdrop (or wider lava) so the world no longer ends in an edge. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--sky-coloured-fog-and-horizon-backdrop-adr-0239))
- Lava that looks molten in the no-code 3D template (ADR-0238): a dark cooled crust broken by thin, glowing, slowly flowing cracks instead of pastel blobs. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--cracked-crust-lava-adr-0238))
- Calibrated lighting in the 3D, no-code 3D and multiplayer templates (ADR-0237): a stronger, lower afternoon sun from the side and exposure 0.6, so new projects start with long readable shadows and no washed-out ground. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--template-lighting-calibration-adr-0237))
- Sun extraction from HDR skies (ADR-0236). The photographed sun in an HDR sky is removed before its ambient and reflections are baked, so it no longer doubles the scene's directional light (washed-out shadows, a second highlight); a scene with only an HDR gets a matching shadowed sun from it, and lightmaps bake under the HDR sky instead of the analytic one. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--sun-extraction-from-hdr-skies-adr-0236))
- IES light profiles (ADR-0235). Manufacturer `.ies` photometric files (LM-63 Type C, all horizontal symmetries) import as texture assets baked into point-light cookie panoramas, so a point light that uses one throws the real fixture's beam: scalloped downlight cones, wall washers and street-lamp patterns. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--ies-light-profiles-adr-0235))
- AgX tonemapper (ADR-0234): Environment `tonemapper: "agx"` gives Blender's default filmic view, which keeps bright saturated colours (fire, neon, sunsets) close to their hue and rolls them towards white instead of skewing them as the ACES fit does. ACES stays the default. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--agx-tonemapper-adr-0234))
- Subsurface scattering for skin, wax, marble and leaves (ADR-0233). `.vmat` `subsurface_color` and `subsurface_thickness` soften and warm the shadow edge (energy-conserving wrapped diffuse) and let light shine through thin parts (Filament's forward transmission lobe, with the sun's transmission shadowed from the far side), plus ambient transmission; Studio material panel Subsurface and Thickness; glTF `KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission` maps onto it (cooked model format 15). ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--subsurface-scattering-adr-0233))
- Anisotropic specular for brushed metal, hair, satin and machined parts (ADR-0232). `.vmat` `anisotropy` (0..1) and `anisotropy_rotation` (radians from the mesh tangent) stretch the GGX highlight along one direction (Burley anisotropic GGX with height-correlated anisotropic Smith visibility, glTF roughness mapping) for the sun, point and spot lights, and bend the environment reflection into a streak; Studio material panel Anisotropy and Anisotropy Rotation; glTF `KHR_materials_anisotropy` factor import (cooked model format 14). ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--anisotropic-specular-adr-0232))
- Sheen materials for cloth and fuzzy surfaces (ADR-0231). `.vmat` `sheen_color` and `sheen_roughness` add the soft grazing-angle rim of velvet, cotton, wool, felt and peach skin (Charlie distribution with Neubelt visibility, energy-scaled base) from the sun, point, spot and sphere lights and the ambient; glTF `KHR_materials_sheen` factors import into model materials (cooked model format 13); Studio material panel Sheen colour and Sheen Roughness. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--sheen-materials-adr-0231))
- Text shaping and right-to-left text in the in-game UI (ADR-0224). UI text is shaped with HarfBuzz and laid out with the Unicode bidirectional algorithm (SheenBidi), so Bengali conjuncts and vowel signs, joined Arabic and Hebrew render correctly, mixed left-to-right and right-to-left lines are reordered, and `UiTextComponent::direction` (auto, ltr, rtl) picks the paragraph direction. Built-in Noto Sans Bengali and Noto Sans Arabic fall back behind Inter per glyph. The glyph atlas starts at 256x256 and grows to 2048x2048 before it recycles, and the whole UI draws with one texture. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--text-shaping-and-bidirectional-layout-adr-0224))
- Mesh particles (ADR-0215). Emitters can draw each particle as a mesh (a built-in primitive or a model's mesh, with an optional material) instead of a billboard: lit, opaque, shadow-casting debris, shards and casings. The meshes tumble with the particle spin, or point along their motion when stretched. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--mesh-particles-adr-0215))
- Facial expressions and equipment on Orion and Nova (ADR-0222). The starter controller has an int parameter `expression` and a `Face` layer that plays the facial clips (0 neutral blink, 1 talk, 2 smile, 3 frown, 4 surprise) over locomotion with 0.25 s cross-fades and without moving the body. Morph targets that move no vertex of a primitive no longer make the renderer copy and upload it: a changing face expression costs 0.14 ms instead of 1.10 ms per frame. Expressions and props held in the sockets are verified in the runtime renderer on Vulkan and GLES. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--facial-expressions-and-equipment-verified-on-orion-and-nova-adr-0222))
- Lit particles (ADR-0214). A new `lit` emitter option makes smoke and dust billboards and trails take the sun (wrapped around the camera-facing normal), the sun's cascaded shadow and the sky/ground ambient, on CPU and GPU emitters. Unlit emitters (fire, sparks, energy) are unchanged. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--lit-particles-sun-shadows-and-sky-ambient-adr-0214))
- Particle simulation budget (ADR-0213). `ParticleSettings::max_cpu_particles` bounds the live particles of all CPU emitters together; the nearest emitters keep spawning and distant ones wait, without popping. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--particle-simulation-budget-nearest-emitters-first-adr-0213))
- Skeleton LOD for distant animators (ADR-0221). Animators with a `SimulationLodComponent` ease finger, toe, jaw, eye and face-morph channels back to rest over `detail_fade` seconds once the entity reaches `AnimatorComponent::detail_lod_tier` (default tier 2, beyond 80 m), then stop sampling them; body, socket and IK joints are unchanged. On Orion, 61 of 96 animation nodes are detail and 64 animators update in 0.27 ms instead of 0.39 ms. `AnimationStats::channels_sampled` now counts only sampled channels. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--skeleton-lod-for-distant-animators-adr-0221))
- Particle overdraw in the overdraw debug view (ADR-0212). View > Overdraw View now counts particle billboards and trails (CPU and GPU emitters), and `SceneRenderer::read_overdraw()` reports the particle share: pixels touched, layers, the worst pixel and fill per screen pixel. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--particle-overdraw-in-the-overdraw-debug-view-adr-0212))
- Particle speed and spin curves over life (ADR-0211). `ParticleEmitter` fields `speed_over_life` and `spin_over_life` scale each particle's movement and spin over its life on CPU and GPU emitters, edited as curves in the Studio Inspector. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--particle-speed-and-spin-curves-over-life-adr-0211))
- Particle simulation LOD (ADR-0210). `ParticleEmitter` fields `cull_distance`, `lod_distance`, `lod_rate`, `lod_spawn_scale` and `offscreen` (Simulate, Throttle, Pause) let distant or off-screen effects update less often, spawn less, pause or sleep, measured from the scene's primary cameras or from viewers set on the `ParticleSystem`. Everything defaults off. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--particle-simulation-lod-adr-0210))
- Particle colour gradients and size curves (ADR-0209). `ParticleEmitter` fields `color_over_life` (colour and alpha keys over each particle's life) and `size_over_life` (Size Start multiplier over life) replace the linear start/end ramps when set, up to 8 keys each, identically on CPU and GPU emitters. Scene files store gradients as `[[time, r, g, b, a], ...]`; the Studio Inspector shows a gradient bar and a curve plot with editable keys. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--particle-colour-gradients-and-size-curves-adr-0209))
- Corrective shapes driven by joint angles (ADR-0220). A model's `correctives` import setting bakes in-between morph targets that make linear skinning match dual quaternion skinning as a joint twists or bends, or drives an authored shape key from a joint angle; the runtime renderer evaluates the drivers from the posed skeleton. On Orion's forearm twisted 150 degrees, collapsed triangles drop from 11 to 0 and the render matches DQS pixel for pixel on Vulkan and GLES. Studio Skeleton card "Corrective shape"; `victus skeleton --corrective joint:axis:degrees`. Cooked model format 12. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--corrective-shapes-driven-by-joint-angles-adr-0220))
- Studio skeleton overlays, pose editing and socket moves (ADR-0208). View > Bones draws the selected character's bones in the live pose, coloured by role; View > Skin Weights shows the Skeleton card's selected bone's weights as a blue-to-red heatmap on the skinned vertices. The card can pose a bone (one undo step) and reset the pose, and "Save transform to model" writes a socket's scene transform as a new `move` skeleton edit in a `.vrig` migration; deforming joints are refused. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--studio-skeleton-overlays-pose-editing-and-socket-moves-adr-0208))
- Reference creature and mechanical rigs (ADR-0207). `assets/reference_rigs.hpp` builds a 38-joint quadruped (tail, jaw, ears, four legs, jiggle belly) and a 13-joint mechanical loader with clips, plus the 19-bone complex-humanoid extension for Orion and Nova; `victus skeleton quadruped|mech` inspects them. GPU-skinned poses of all three, and of Orion under extreme poses, match the CPU-skinned reference in the runtime renderer on Vulkan and GLES. CPU dual quaternion skinning is 2.6 times faster (Orion body 8.9 to 3.4 ms). ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--reference-creature-and-mechanical-rigs-verified-in-the-runtime-renderer-adr-0207))
- Dual quaternion skinning and skeleton extension (ADR-0206). `SkinnedMeshComponent::method` `dual_quaternion` keeps twisted forearms and bent elbows from collapsing, on every GPU backend and in shadows and motion vectors; a CPU skinner, weight analysis, normalisation, influence limiting, weight heatmaps and deformation metrics come with it. `assets/skeleton.hpp` inspects skeletons (bone roles, sockets, layout hash) and adds, renames, reparents and removes bones without changing existing joint indices or deformation, with versioned JSON skeleton migrations (`.vrig`, listed in a model's `skeleton_migrations` import setting) and a compatibility check. `victus skeleton` prints a model's bones and checks migrations; the Studio Inspector's "Skeleton and skinning" card switches the skinning method, measures deformation and adds bones as migrations. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--dual-quaternion-skinning-and-skeleton-extension-adr-0206))
- Cloth sheets on Jolt soft bodies (ADR-0205). The `Cloth` component simulates a rectangular sheet (flags, banners, curtains, capes) with pins that follow the entity (top edge, top corners, left edge, corners), stretch/shear/bend compliance, damping, friction, thickness, collisions with rigid bodies and aerodynamic wind with gusts; the simulated sheet is drawn through the entity's procedural mesh. Luau: `entity:cloth_state`, `entity:cloth_impulse`, `entity:set_wind`. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--cloth-sheets-on-jolt-soft-bodies-adr-0205))
- Point and directional light cookies (ADR-0201). `LightComponent::cookie` now also wraps a texture around a point light as a panorama (lamp shades, cage lanterns) and tiles one across the world from the sun, `cookie_size` metres per repeat (leaf dapple, window frames, caustics); `DirectionalLight::cookie_offset_x/y` scrolls it. Studio Inspector Cookie field on point and directional lights; `victus check` warns only on area-light cookies. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--point-and-directional-light-cookies-adr-0201))
- Clear coat materials (ADR-0203). `.vmat` `clearcoat` (0..1) and `clearcoat_roughness` add a glossy transparent layer over the base (lacquer, car paint, glazed ceramic, visors) with its own sharp reflection from the sun, point, spot and sphere lights and the environment or probes; glTF `KHR_materials_clearcoat` factors import into model materials (cooked model format 11); Studio material panel Clear Coat and Coat Roughness sliders. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--clear-coat-materials-and-helmeted-frame-redesign-adr-0203-adr-0204))
- Screen-space reflections on opaque glossy surfaces (ADR-0199). Polished floors, metal and other surfaces smoother than `RenderSettings::ssr_max_roughness` (default 0.5) trace reflections in the forward shader against last frame's TAA colour and depth history, falling back to probes and the environment; High and Ultra (TAA) on Vulkan, Metal and D3D12; the render path report lists `ssr_opaque`. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--screen-space-reflections-on-opaque-glossy-surfaces-adr-0199))
- Procedural city from one seed (ADR-0202). New `procgen` module and `City` component: an irregular road grid with avenues, kerbed blocks and sidewalks, lots, buildings that rise towards downtown with setback towers, parks, street lights and trees, generated on the first simulation tick and drawn as one vertex-coloured mesh per cell and layer. It also builds optional static colliders, a traffic lane graph with `vehicles` agents and `pedestrians` CrowdAgents walking the sidewalks. Generated entities are marked with the new `TransientComponent` and are not saved; the scene keeps only the settings. A 12×12 city (1,018 buildings, 208k triangles) generates in 0.3 ms and meshes in under 30 ms. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--procedural-city-from-one-seed-adr-0202))
- Crowd agents (ADR-0200). The `CrowdAgent` component draws a whole skinned model from its clips, baked once per (model, skin, clip) into a GPU palette buffer: no joint entities and no per-agent pose evaluation or upload, and agents playing the same clip share one instanced draw per mesh. Fields: `model`, `skin`, `clip`, `time`, `speed`, `playing`, `loop`, `tint`, `cast_shadows`. Verified on Vulkan and GLES with Mesa software rasterisers. 1,000 agents of the 2-joint test rig take 0.19 ms of CPU per frame against 0.90 ms as Animator characters. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--crowd-agents-draw-baked-skin-palettes-adr-0200))
- World-space triplanar materials and CC0 starter content (ADR-0197). `.vmat` `triplanar` / `triplanar_sharpness` project every texture from world position (`uv_scale` = repeats per metre), so textures tile evenly on scaled blocks with no stretching; Studio material panel Triplanar checkbox and Blend Sharpness slider. The 3D and No-Code 3D templates now ship five 1K CC0 PBR materials (grass, rock, planks, metal, paving) and a CC0 HDR sky used for the background and image-based lighting; the No-Code 3D level's scenery uses them instead of flat colours. The Robot Arena sample uses them too (paving floor, rock walls and pillars, plank cover, HDR sky), and so do the 3D template's ground and the Multiplayer template's arena (paving floor, rock walls, steel pillars, HDR sky). A mesh-UV `crate` material (planks) textures the 3D template's crates and swinging gate, which move and so cannot use world triplanar. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--world-space-triplanar-materials-and-cc0-starter-content-adr-0197))
- Screen-space reflections on water (ADR-0196). Water mirrors the objects around it on Medium, High and Ultra (16 / 32 / 48 ray steps), fading to the environment reflection off screen and at the screen edges. `RenderSettings::screen_space_reflections`, `ssr_steps`, `ssr_max_distance`, `ssr_thickness`; the render path report lists `ssr`. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--screen-space-reflections-on-water-adr-0196))
- Spot light cookies (ADR-0195). `LightComponent` field `cookie` projects a texture asset through a spot light's cone (window frames, leaf shadows, stained glass), shaping its light on surfaces, its light shafts in volumetric fog and its baked light in lightmaps. `SceneRenderer::set_cookie` keeps up to 64 cookies in a storage-buffer atlas; Studio Inspector Cookie field; `victus check` reports missing cookies and cookies on other light types. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--spot-light-cookies-adr-0195))
- Rectangle area lights, bulb radius and Baked lights (ADR-0194). `LightComponent` type `area` with `width`/`height` lights one side of a rectangle (windows, screens, softboxes) with exact diffuse irradiance and representative-point glossy highlights; point lights gain `radius` for wider, softer highlights; `baked` moves a point, spot or area light's direct light and soft shadows into the lightmap and probes. Studio: Add > Area Light, Inspector fields and a viewport outline. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--rectangle-area-lights-bulb-radius-and-baked-lights-adr-0194))

### Changed

- Faster CPU frames for large scenes (ADR-0226 to ADR-0230). Transforms propagate only from changed entities and `parallel_for` claims work in batches (ADR-0226); materials are interned per frame and draws radix-sorted by ranked state keys (ADR-0227); per-draw visibility is classified in parallel on the job system with a serial in-order gather (ADR-0228); materials are compared by packed signatures and `victus_bench_runtime` measures whole runtime frames (ADR-0229); unchanged static mesh renderers are retained in automatic instance groups (`RenderSettings::retain_static_draws`, ADR-0230). On 10,000 static cubes in the 2-vCPU sandbox a runtime frame's CPU time drops from about 4.0 ms to about 1.2-1.3 ms with retention on. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--retained-static-draws-adr-0230))
- Orion and Nova follow the design sheet more closely (ADR-0225): smaller heads under rounder helmets and longer legs; Nova slimmer, with smaller shoulder caps, narrower thigh plates, graphite knees and pearl boots; Orion with a six-pack abdomen, a narrower belt, slimmer pauldrons and thighs and ceramic boot straps; both with hollow eye glyphs, a mouth glyph and a graphite frame around the visor. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-23--design-sheet-proportions-and-detail-pass-adr-0225))
- Orion's shoulders are layered (a second shell on each pauldron) and Nova's thighs carry raised outer panels with a lit groove (ADR-0225). ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--orion-layered-pauldrons-and-nova-thigh-panels-adr-0225))
- Orion and Nova materials (ADR-0225): graphite composite plates are a satin mid-graphite, distinct from the near-black undersuit, and the visors are blue (Orion) or violet (Nova) tinted glass with a faint inner light. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--orion-and-nova-materials-graphite-composite-and-visor-inner-light-adr-0225))
- Orion and Nova armour plates are hard-surface parts (ADR-0225): their outer surfaces are refit as smooth panels so anatomy no longer shows through, rims are subdivided as creases with split shading, and Orion gets faceted pauldrons, split thigh plates, white kneecaps, upper-arm plates, a segmented spine and a larger back reactor; Nova gets white ceramic boots. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--orion-and-nova-armour-hard-surface-plates-and-component-rebuild-adr-0225))
- Orion and Nova armour rebuilt as synthetic construction, first components (ADR-0225): Orion's chest is one angular ceramic breastplate with a chevron edge over four separate graphite abdominal lames; the helmets get a face-shaped smoked-glass visor from brow to chin that wraps to the ear pods, with a cyan or violet rim glow and arc eye glyphs, and brushed-metal side panels; plates are faired, crisp-edged mouldings and the undersuit loses its muscle detail. Subdivision now treats non-manifold edges as creases. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--orion-and-nova-armour-architecture-torso-helmet-and-visor-adr-0225))
- Orion and Nova get a new base body (ADR-0223): a heroic athletic Orion and a slender Nova, less smoothed so the muscle forms show, in a graphite undersuit of moulded panels that follow the muscles with thin cyan or violet light lines (abdomen sides into the hips, under the pectorals or bust, the shoulder blades, the flanks), plus sculpted armour plates grown from outlines on body charts. Subdivision no longer throws non-manifold boundary vertices off the surface. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--orion-and-nova-base-body-and-muscle-panelled-undersuit-adr-0223))
- Orion and Nova redesigned as Frame 01 and Frame 02 (ADR-0204): a smooth helmet with a wraparound black glass visor carrying the eye and mouth glyphs, thicker armour plates (25, with new calf plates, larger shoulders and a wider chest and back), Voronoi panel lines, graphite inserts and metal fasteners, and clear-coated ceramic and glass. Nova is now pearl ceramic with silver hardware and violet light instead of graphite, copper and amber. Heights are to the top of the helmet (1.78 m and 1.69 m) and `Socket.Head` sits on the helmet; the Robot Arena sample's copies and socket transforms are updated. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--clear-coat-materials-and-helmeted-frame-redesign-adr-0203-adr-0204))
- Orion and Nova are now Frame mannequins (ADR-0198): armoured sci-fi test dummies instead of realistic human pilots. Each has a smoothed undersuit body with a hex micro-weave, ribbed flex zones and lit piping; 23 satin-ceramic armour plates with bevels, inset light grooves, stripes and a chest emblem; boots, ear pods and a glowing back core; and a glass visor whose emissive eye and mouth glyphs are driven by the same ARKit-named blend shapes (26 now), so the facial clips animate a face display. Three materials (Frame, Armor, Visor; base colour, normal, ORM and emissive each) instead of six. Skeleton, clips, sockets, asset paths and UUIDs are unchanged. The separate `<Name>_Boots/Gloves/Belt/Backpack` equipment nodes are removed. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-22--orion-and-nova-become-frame-mannequins-adr-0198))
- Faster first frame for animated models. The animation system now reads only the skeleton, clips and morph names from a cooked model, skipping texture and mesh data, and loose cooked files are memory-mapped (`fs::map_file`). The model and texture loaders no longer copy the whole file before decoding, and collision meshes skip textures. Measured on the 3D template (Release, 2 vCPU, lavapipe): the first frame's `system.animation.update` dropped from 455-597 ms to 71-81 ms, and a two-frame `victus run` from 3.1-3.3 s to 2.5-2.8 s. Peak memory is unchanged at about 840 MB.
- Asset up-to-date checks cache each source file's hash per path, size and modification time instead of re-reading it on every load. Files modified in the last 3 seconds are always re-hashed. With this, the 3D template's first-frame animation update is 9.3-9.6 ms (it was 71-81 ms after the decode change above), and a two-frame `victus run` takes 2.35-2.44 s.
- The renderer decodes only the compressed texture variant this GPU will upload (`assets::VariantFilter`), keeping the first variant the device samples and skipping the rest without copying. A desktop no longer holds the ASTC copy of a character's textures, and a phone no longer holds a BC7 copy. Textures the device cannot sample keep every variant for CPU decode. On the 3D template, decoding one starter character now takes 41-53 ms instead of 124-191 ms (75 MB of the 150 MB of compressed texture data is skipped). The rendered frame is pixel-identical.
- Exported games read each model's skeleton and clips from a small `.vskel` pack entry written by export (`assets::make_skeleton_sidecar`), instead of decompressing the whole cooked model for animation. On the 3D template's Android pack, the first-frame animation update takes 8.6-8.9 ms instead of 150-168 ms, and each starter character is decompressed once at startup instead of twice; the pack grows by 0.3 MB. Packs exported before this change still load (the full model is read). Pack reads have `pack.read`, `pack.verify` and `pack.decompress` profile zones.
- glTF animation clips are keyframe-reduced on import (`animation::reduce_keyframes`, model import settings `keyframe_reduction`, `keyframe_rotation_error` in degrees, default 0.05, and relative `keyframe_error`, default 0.001). Linear keys that interpolation reproduces within those errors are dropped; first and last keys stay. The starter characters' skeleton and clip data goes from 1.8 MB to 0.4 MB each. The model importer version is now 9, so models re-import once.
- Starter character suits: the chest outline is now a tape instead of emissive piping, and the emissive strength drops from 4 to 2 (ADR-0185). Character GLBs grow to about 20 MB and about 205k LOD 0 triangles with equipment. Existing mesh, material and entity indices are unchanged. ([evidence](docs/STATUS.md#2026-11-21--starter-character-equipment-adr-0185))
